El Cajon sits in a hot inland valley ringed by hills east of La Mesa. Most homes are 1950s to 1970s ranch houses, many with pools and room for an RV, plus hillside homes in Fletcher Hills and Granite Hills and bigger lots toward Crest and Rancho San Diego. What I look at in El Cajon

  1. 1

    El Cajon summers make pools common, so I look at the pool's age and condition in your photos, since a newly plastered pool and a cracked, empty one affect value differently.

  2. 2

  3. 3

    On the hillsides of Fletcher Hills and Granite Hills, I weigh views across the valley against steep driveways, boulders and slopes that eat into usable yard space.

What to photograph

Photograph the pool and equipment, the RV gate or side yard, and the roof if it's visible from the yard. On a hillside lot, show the driveway, the view across the valley, and how much flat yard you really have.

El Cajon questions

My brother is executor of Mom's El Cajon house. Can he order the appraisal himself?
Yes. The executor or the estate's attorney usually orders it, and I can value the house as of the date of death, today, or both. In probate the referee also values it for the court, but my report with El Cajon sales and your photos often helps plan a sale or split things fairly.
Our El Cajon house has a bad roof and a cracked pool. Does that hurt value?
I look at what buyers in El Cajon have paid for houses with similar problems, not just the repair bids. Send photos of the roof, the ceiling stains and the pool, plus any estimates, and I'll describe each issue in the report so everyone sees how I handled it.
