What it's for

Twelve reasons people order one

Date-of-death valuations come first because they're the most common, but they're far from the only use.

  • Date of death & step-up in basis

    Heirs usually receive a fresh tax basis equal to the home's value on the day the owner died. The appraisal pins down that figure, so capital gains are calculated correctly when the house sells, even if the death was years ago.

  • Trusts & estates

    Trustees and executors need a value they can defend: for an accounting, for dividing things fairly, or for selling the house to one of the beneficiaries. A signed appraisal answers the question of where the number came from.

  • Divorce & separation

    Whether one of you keeps the house or it gets sold, settlement talks move faster with an independent value. I can value the home as of today, the date of separation, or both.

  • Family sales & buyouts

    Buying out a brother's share, selling to your daughter, or splitting a house with a former partner? The appraisal gives everyone the same starting point and records a fair price.

  • Gift tax

    Deeding a house, or part of one, to a relative is a gift with a value that gets reported. The appraisal supports the figure on the federal gift-tax return, Form 709.

  • Prop 19 transfers

    When a parent's home passes to a child, Prop 19 limits how much of the old assessment carries over. Market value on the transfer date shows where you stand with the county assessor.

  • Property tax appeals

    If the county assesses your home above what it would sell for, an independent appraisal is the evidence for a decline-in-value review or a formal appeal.

  • Bankruptcy

    Your attorney needs a supportable value for the schedules and the homestead exemption. Problems with the house get documented from your photos, so they count.

  • Before you sell

    Set the asking price with an opinion from someone who isn't trying to win your listing.

  • Before you buy

    Paying cash, buying from family, or skipping the appraisal contingency? Check the price while you can still walk away.

  • Immigration sponsors (I-864)

    Sponsors who count home equity on the Affidavit of Support are asked for a recent appraisal by a licensed appraiser. This is that appraisal.

  • Bail & property bonds

    Courts that take a property bond want proof of the equity, usually from a licensed appraiser. A current report keeps the bond paperwork moving.
